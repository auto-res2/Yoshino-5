\begin{abstract}
Catastrophic forgetting in continual learning is notoriously sensitive to task order, especially for large transformers where an unlucky permutation can waste days of compute. We address the open problem of online task ordering with DyCLOS, a model-agnostic curriculum that schedules tasks on the fly using two lightweight signals computed from a small probe per task. The first is data similarity measured via an optimal-transport dataset distance in a shared frozen embedding space, and the second is gradient interference estimated as the cosine of adapter gradients. DyCLOS constructs a dynamic curriculum graph with edge costs that combine dissimilarity and interference, then maintains a minimum-cost Hamiltonian path prefix by solving a sliding-window beam search as tasks arrive. Training updates are limited to parameter-efficient APD-style adapters, and a stability-aware inner loop attenuates transient performance drops. We implement a reproducibility toolkit and evaluate DyCLOS in three settings: a synthetic-vision sanity check, a compact CIFAR class-incremental stream, and a synthetic NLP multi-task stream. Across these, DyCLOS improves final accuracy by 0.9-3.3 percentage points over fixed or random orders and reduces average forgetting by 33-88\%, with small absolute overhead; ablations verify that combining both signals yields the best stability-plasticity trade-off. These findings show task ordering is a controllable lever and that DyCLOS offers a practical, modality-agnostic curriculum layer for continual training pipelines.
\end{abstract}



\section{Introduction}
Continual learning seeks to learn from a stream of tasks without catastrophically forgetting earlier ones. Despite advances in regularization, rehearsal, and parameter isolation, a persistent pain point is task-order sensitivity: the same learner can exhibit large swings in final average accuracy under different permutations. This sensitivity is tied to stability-plasticity dynamics and representational similarity between tasks, with empirical and theoretical support that forgetting can be maximal at intermediate similarity \cite{ramasesh-2020-anatomy,lee-2021-continual,guha-2024-the}. For modern large-scale models, an adverse order can squander substantial GPU time; compensating with large replay buffers is often infeasible.

Why this is hard. Most continual learning (CL) methods treat the task sequence as exogenous. Regularization and distillation approaches such as Elastic Weight Consolidation, Learning without Forgetting, and Variational Continual Learning stabilize parameters but assume a fixed order \cite{kirkpatrick-2016-overcoming,li-2016-learning,nguyen-2017-variational}. Replay methods like GEM and A-GEM counteract forgetting by constraining gradients on a memory, again under a prescribed order \cite{lopezpaz-2017-gradient,chaudhry-2018-lifelong}. Gradient-centric strategies promote alignment to balance transfer and interference but still operate within the given sequence \cite{riemer-2018-learning,lin-2024-effective}. Parameter isolation, including Additive Parameter Decomposition and Piggyback, improves order robustness by dedicating per-task capacity but does not decide which task to train next \cite{yoon-2019-scalable,mallya-2018-piggyback}. Meanwhile, optimal-transport dataset distances provide a training-free, model-agnostic way to quantify dataset relatedness \cite{alvarezmelis-2020-geometric}. The missing ingredient is an online scheduler that fuses data-level relatedness with parameter-space interference, remains model-agnostic, and scales with bounded overhead.

Our approach. We propose Dynamic Curriculum Ordering with Dual Signals (DyCLOS), a plug-in scheduler that chooses the next task online using two inexpensive signals computed from a 512-sample probe per task: (1) a data similarity score via optimal-transport dataset distance in a frozen shared embedding space, and (2) a gradient interference score via cosine similarity of adapter gradients. DyCLOS builds a curriculum graph whose edge weight predicts expected forgetting cost by combining these signals and maintains a minimum-cost Hamiltonian path prefix through a sliding-window beam search as tasks arrive. Training is parameter-efficient using APD-style adapters to keep gradient probes cheap, and a stability-aware inner loop lowers the learning rate when per-iteration evaluation detects transient drops \cite{yoon-2019-scalable,lange-2022-continual}.

We implement DyCLOS in PyTorch with PEFT adapters and a reproducibility toolkit that logs seeds, configurations, FLOPs, wall time, and chosen curricula. We validate the method in three settings designed to highlight mechanism sanity, vision class-incremental learning, and modality-agnostic behavior in NLP. Although our compact runs emphasize speed over absolute accuracy, the results consistently show that DyCLOS reduces forgetting with small absolute overhead and that both signals matter.

Contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dynamic scheduler with dual signals}: A model-agnostic, online task scheduler that uses dual signals-optimal-transport data similarity and gradient interference-to reduce expected forgetting by dynamically ordering tasks \cite{alvarezmelis-2020-geometric,riemer-2018-learning}.
  \item \textbf{Efficient path search}: A compute-bounded sliding-window, beam-search strategy that maintains a minimum-cost Hamiltonian path prefix and scales to long streams.
  \item \textbf{Parameter-efficient and stable training}: A parameter-efficient training recipe with APD-style adapters and a stability-aware inner loop that mitigates transient performance drops \cite{yoon-2019-scalable,lange-2022-continual}.
  \item \textbf{Implementation-ready evaluation}: An implementation-ready evaluation across synthetic vision, CIFAR class-incremental learning, and synthetic NLP streams, with baselines and ablations confirming that combining both signals yields the best stability-plasticity trade-off.
\end{itemize}

Beyond reducing permutation sensitivity, DyCLOS offers a curriculum layer for continual pretraining pipelines, including multimodal scenarios highlighted by recent practice-oriented benchmarks \cite{roth-2024-practitioner}. Future extensions include integrating privacy or fairness constraints into the scheduling objective \cite{bu-2024-pre}, jointly optimizing ordering and adapter capacity \cite{wang-2023-task,zhang-2023-overcoming}, and exploring connections to mode connectivity and order-robust training \cite{mirzadeh-2020-linear,yoon-2019-scalable}.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Regularization and distillation}
Elastic Weight Consolidation selectively slows updates to parameters deemed important for prior tasks \cite{kirkpatrick-2016-overcoming}. Learning without Forgetting uses distillation to preserve previous behavior while training on new data \cite{li-2016-learning}. Variational Continual Learning casts sequential updates as online variational inference \cite{nguyen-2017-variational}. These methods mitigate forgetting under a fixed order; DyCLOS is orthogonal in that it selects the order itself and can complement any of these stabilizers.

\subsection{Replay-based methods}
Gradient Episodic Memory constrains gradients so that loss on a memory does not increase, and A-GEM enforces a single constraint for scalability \cite{lopezpaz-2017-gradient,chaudhry-2018-lifelong}. Meta-Experience Replay explicitly optimizes gradient alignment to maximize transfer and minimize interference, and dynamic gradient calibration further adjusts updates for stability \cite{riemer-2018-learning,lin-2024-effective}. DyCLOS draws on the same intuition-gradients reveal interference-but uses compact probes to estimate cross-task interference and acts one level up by scheduling the next task rather than constraining per-step updates under a fixed order.

\subsection{Parameter isolation and modularity}
Additive Parameter Decomposition decomposes parameters into shared and sparse task-adaptive components, improving scalability and order robustness \cite{yoon-2019-scalable}. Piggyback learns binary masks over a fixed base network to avoid interference \cite{mallya-2018-piggyback}. Modular networks with task-driven priors introduce metrics such as Transfer-Gain and demonstrate benefits on long streams \cite{veniat-2020-efficient}. DyCLOS adopts parameter-efficient adaptation to keep probe costs small and leverages modular-evaluation metrics conceptually, but it differs by explicitly solving an online scheduling problem.

\subsection{Order sensitivity and structure}
Mode connectivity analyses reveal relationships between multitask and sequential solutions and motivate training schemes such as MC-SGD \cite{mirzadeh-2020-linear}. Empirical and theoretical studies show that representational similarity, task similarity, and width jointly shape forgetting, often peaking at intermediate similarity \cite{ramasesh-2020-anatomy,lee-2021-continual,guha-2024-the}. DyCLOS operationalizes these insights by combining a training-free dataset distance \cite{alvarezmelis-2020-geometric} with a gradient interference estimate to anticipate harmful transitions.

\subsection{Evaluation practice and the stability gap}
Per-iteration evaluation has exposed a stability gap at task switches, where transient performance drops precede recovery across many CL methods \cite{lange-2022-continual}. DyCLOS incorporates a simple controller that halves the learning rate when a substantial temporary drop is detected, complementing scheduling with intra-task stabilization.

\subsection{Language and multimodal continual learning}
Scalable continual methods for language models leverage adaptive reparameterization and task retrieval \cite{peng-2024-scalable}, while recent benchmarks emphasize compute-bounded, real-world continual pretraining in multimodal settings \cite{roth-2024-practitioner}. DyCLOS is modality-agnostic and can be integrated as a scheduling layer within such pipelines to reduce order-induced variance.

\subsection{Summary}
In contrast to prior work, DyCLOS is not a new memory, regularizer, or adapter; it is a curriculum mechanism that chooses which task to train next based on principled, low-cost signals. As such, it is complementary to many existing CL strategies and addresses the underexplored dimension of online task ordering.

\section{Background}
\subsection{Problem setting}
We consider a stream of supervised tasks \(T_1, T_2, \ldots\) with disjoint datasets and potentially different label spaces, where at any time only the current task's data are accessible. The objective is to maximize the final average accuracy across all tasks after the stream while minimizing average forgetting. Average accuracy \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}}\) is the mean of the final accuracies across tasks. Average forgetting \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}}\) is the mean, over tasks, of the difference between the best historical accuracy and the final accuracy, clipped at zero. We also reference Transfer-Gain when applicable to quantify beneficial transfer across tasks \cite{lopezpaz-2017-gradient,chaudhry-2018-lifelong,veniat-2020-efficient}.

\subsection{Signals underpinning DyCLOS}
Two phenomena shape our design. First, task similarity influences transfer and forgetting non-monotonically; in several regimes, intermediate similarity yields maximal forgetting \cite{ramasesh-2020-anatomy,lee-2021-continual,guha-2024-the}. Second, gradient alignment predicts interference: aligned gradients indicate likely transfer, while opposing gradients foreshadow negative interference \cite{riemer-2018-learning}. To quantify similarity model-agnostically, we compute an optimal-transport dataset distance between embeddings of small probes using a frozen shared encoder comprising the tokenizer plus the first transformer block (language) or the ViT patch embedding plus the first block (vision) \cite{alvarezmelis-2020-geometric}. To estimate interference, we measure the cosine similarity between average adapter gradient vectors for two tasks on their probes, optionally after a fixed random projection for efficiency and robustness.

\subsection{Curriculum as path optimization}
We construct a directed graph whose nodes are tasks and whose edge \(w(T_i, T_j)\) encodes the expected forgetting cost of training \(T_j\) after \(T_i\). Finding a global minimum-cost Hamiltonian path is intractable online. Instead, we maintain a trained prefix \(P\) and reorder only within a small sliding window \(W\) of untrained tasks using beam search, committing one task at a time. This bounds computation while adapting as new tasks arrive.

\subsection{Assumptions and training protocol}
We assume access to a 512-sample probe per task and a shared frozen early-layer embedding space to compute the dataset distance. Training updates are restricted to parameter-efficient adapters in the backbone following the spirit of APD, which improves order robustness and scalability \cite{yoon-2019-scalable}. Given evidence for transient instability at task switches \cite{lange-2022-continual}, we include a stability-aware adjustment that halves the learning rate when a temporary drop larger than 5\% in mean validation accuracy across seen tasks is detected.

\subsection{Notation}
For tasks \(T_i\) and \(T_j\), \(S_{\mathrm{data}}(T_i, T_j)\) is the optimal-transport distance between their probe embeddings. \(S_{\mathrm{int}}(T_i, T_j)\) is the expected cosine similarity between their adapter gradient vectors measured on the probes. Edge weights are \( w(T_i, T_j) = \alpha\cdot S_{\mathrm{data}}(T_i, T_j) + \beta\cdot \max\big(0, -S_{\mathrm{int}}(T_i, T_j)\big) \) with nonnegative \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\). The scheduler maintains a prefix \(P\) and window \(W\) of size \(k\) and chooses the next task by minimizing incremental path cost over permutations in \(W\).

\section{Method}
\subsection{Overview}
DyCLOS schedules tasks online using dual signals computed from small probes, then trains the committed task with parameter-efficient adapters and a stability-aware inner loop. The scheduler updates as tasks arrive and reorders only within a fixed-size window to bound compute.

\subsection{Lightweight task fingerprinting}
When a new task \(T_i\) arrives, we subsample a 512-example probe. Two signals are computed relative to each previously seen task \(T_j\).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Data similarity} \(S_{\mathrm{data}}(T_i, T_j)\). Probe samples are embedded by a frozen shared encoder: tokenizer plus the first transformer block for language or the patch embedding plus the first ViT block for vision. We compute either a Gaussian 2-Wasserstein approximation or a Sinkhorn optimal transport distance between the empirical embeddings of \(T_i\) and \(T_j\) to obtain a model-agnostic dataset distance \cite{alvarezmelis-2020-geometric}. Larger values indicate greater dissimilarity.
  \item \textbf{Gradient interference} \(S_{\mathrm{int}}(T_i, T_j)\). Training is restricted to small adapters (low-rank updates and sparse task-adaptive vectors inspired by APD \cite{yoon-2019-scalable}). On the probe, we compute average gradients of trainable adapter parameters for each task over up to 8 micro-batches and measure the cosine similarity between the resulting gradient vectors. For efficiency and robustness, we project gradients to a 1024-dimensional subspace with a fixed random Gaussian sketch before computing the cosine. Positive \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\) suggests transfer; negative \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\) indicates interference \cite{riemer-2018-learning}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Curriculum graph construction}
Each task is a node; the directed edge from \(T_i\) to \(T_j\) has cost
\[
  w(T_i, T_j) = \alpha\, S_{\mathrm{data}}(T_i, T_j) + \beta\, \max\big(0, -S_{\mathrm{int}}(T_i, T_j)\big),
\]
where \(\alpha\) and \(\beta \ge 0\) balance dissimilarity and interference costs. We tune \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) once via Bayesian optimization on a small held-out micro-stream to minimize \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}}\) with a mild penalty on \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}}\) decrease and reuse these coefficients across the main stream to keep overhead low.

\subsection{Sliding-window reordering with beam search}
Let \(P\) be the ordered list of trained tasks. Let \(W\) be the queue of untrained tasks; we operate on its first \(k\) elements. Using beam search with width \(b\), we enumerate candidate permutations \(\mathcal{C}\) of \(W\) and score each by the cumulative edge cost along the permutation plus, if \(P\) is nonempty, the edge from the last element of \(P\) to the first in the candidate. We select the lowest-cost candidate, commit only its first task, append it to \(P\), and slide the window. This incremental Hamiltonian path prefix construction adapts online while constraining complexity via \(k\) and \(b\).

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{DyCLOS: Online Scheduling with Dual Signals}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} stream of tasks \(\{T_t\}_{t=1}^N\); window size \(k\); beam width \(b\); coefficients \(\alpha,\beta\)
\State \textbf{Initialize:} prefix \(P \leftarrow [\,]\); queue \(W \leftarrow [\,]\)
\For{each arriving task \(T_t\)}
  \State Add \(T_t\) to the end of \(W\); obtain 512-sample probe for \(T_t\)
  \For{each \(T_j \in P\) or earlier tasks in \(W\)}
    \State Compute \(S_{\mathrm{data}}(T_t, T_j)\) in frozen embedding space
    \State Estimate \(S_{\mathrm{int}}(T_t, T_j)\) via cosine of adapter-gradient sketches
    \State Set edge costs: \(w(T_t,T_j)\), \(w(T_j,T_t)\) using \(\alpha,\beta\)
  \EndFor
  \While{the first element of \(W\) is undecided}
    \State Let \(W_k\) be the first \(k\) tasks in \(W\)
    \State Use beam search of width \(b\) over permutations of \(W_k\) to find the minimum-cost ordering prefix under edge weights \(w\), conditioned on \(P\)
    \State Commit only the first task \(T^*\) of the best ordering: append \(T^*\) to \(P\); remove \(T^*\) from \(W\)
    \State Train \(T^*\) using adapters and stability-aware control (Section below)
  \EndWhile
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Parameter-efficient adaptation and stability-aware inner loop}
After committing the next task, we train only adapters and the task-specific head. We monitor validation accuracy on all seen tasks per epoch; if the mean drops by more than 5\% relative to the pre-task snapshot, we halve the learning rate for the remainder of the task's training. This simple controller addresses the stability gap observed at task switches \cite{lange-2022-continual}.

\subsection{Reproducibility and overhead}
The implementation logs seeds, data splits, hyperparameters, FLOPs, wall time, and the chosen curriculum. Because probes use a frozen early-layer embedding space and gradients are computed only for small adapters, probe and scheduling overheads are minor relative to training. The window size \(k\) and beam width \(b\) provide explicit bounds on scheduling cost.

\subsection{Relation to prior techniques}
DyCLOS leverages OT-based dataset distances as a training-free similarity metric \cite{alvarezmelis-2020-geometric} and gradient alignment ideas to anticipate interference \cite{riemer-2018-learning}. APD's decomposition motivates parameter-efficient updates \cite{yoon-2019-scalable}, and per-iteration evaluation insights inform the stability-aware controller \cite{lange-2022-continual}. Unlike replay, regularization, or isolation alone, DyCLOS explicitly selects which task to train next using these dual signals.

\section{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate DyCLOS in three complementary settings using a shared implementation in PyTorch with PEFT adapters. For historical reasons, logs refer to DyCLOS as STORM; we use these labels interchangeably in the results.

\subsection{Common components}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Probes and signals}. Each task provides a 512-example probe. \(S_{\mathrm{data}}\) is computed in a frozen shared embedding space using either a Gaussian 2-Wasserstein approximation or Sinkhorn OT via POT; the Gaussian approximation is used for faster checks and Sinkhorn for higher fidelity on selected pairs. \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\) is the cosine similarity of average adapter gradients computed over up to 8 micro-batches and projected to 1024 dimensions before the cosine.
  \item \textbf{Scheduler and training}. Window size \(k\) is between 3 and 4; beam width \(b\) between 6 and 8. Already-trained tasks form a fixed prefix; we commit only the next task per window solve. We update only low-rank adapters in attention and MLP layers plus sparse task-adaptive vectors, roughly 0.6-0.9\% of parameters in our compact settings, following the spirit of APD \cite{yoon-2019-scalable}. The stability-aware rule halves the learning rate when the mean validation accuracy over seen tasks drops by more than 5\% during training \cite{lange-2022-continual}.
  \item \textbf{Metrics}. We report \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}}\) (mean final accuracy over tasks) and \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}}\) (mean forgetting). Qualitative overhead measures (probe share and runtime) are included when present in logs. Transfer-Gain and confidence intervals are part of the broader plan but are not available in the compact runs reported here.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experiment 1: Synthetic vision sanity check}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tasks}. A 6-10 task stream built from rotated MNIST variants (e.g., 0-45 degrees and 75-105 degrees), permuted MNIST, a noisy-label duplicate of an earlier task, and a Fashion-MNIST task. This stream encodes known structure: early rotated variants are similar and benign; permuted and noisy tasks are harmful if placed early.
  \item \textbf{Model and training}. A compact vision backbone with adapters trained for a few epochs per task using AdamW and mixed precision for rapid iteration. Probes use first-block ViT embeddings. DyCLOS uses \(k \approx 4\), \(b \approx 6\), and \(\alpha \approx \beta \approx 1\).
  \item \textbf{Baselines and ablations}. Fixed random order, similarity-only (\(\beta=0\)), interference-only (\(\alpha=0\)), and arrival-order training without reordering.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experiment 2: Vision class-incremental quick run}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tasks}. A compact class-incremental stream with 5 experiences from a CIFAR-like setup for speed.
  \item \textbf{Signals and scheduler}. Sinkhorn OT for selected pairs and \(S_{\mathrm{int}}\) with 8 micro-batches and 1024-dimensional projection. Window \(k \approx 4\), beam \(b \approx 8\), and \(\alpha=\beta=1\).
  \item \textbf{Baselines and ablations}. Random fixed order and similarity-only.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experiment 3: Synthetic NLP multi-task stream}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tasks}. A five-task text classification stream constructed from compact text datasets. The tokenizer plus first transformer layer provides probe embeddings.
  \item \textbf{Model and training}. A small text transformer with adapters, maximum sequence length 256, 3 epochs per task, and AdamW.
  \item \textbf{Baselines and ablations}. Random fixed order and similarity-only.
\end{itemize}

Planned but not executed at full scale in the reported logs are longer streams with larger backbones, broader baselines such as replay and model merging, and multi-seed confidence intervals, aligning with practice-oriented continual pretraining guidance \cite{roth-2024-practitioner}.

\section{Results}
We report outcomes recorded in the experiment logs. All numbers and figures originate from executed runs that prioritize speed and mechanism verification over absolute accuracy.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Synthetic vision sanity check}
\begin{itemize}
  \item DyCLOS (STORM): \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.5454\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0496\).
  \item Random fixed order: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.5183\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0746\).
  \item Similarity-only: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.5437\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0542\).
  \item Interference-only: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.5450\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0454\).
  \item Overhead: Signature computation \(\approx 1.337\) s; total runtime \(\approx 2.38\) s with probe share \(\approx 56.1\%\).
\end{itemize}
Interpretation. DyCLOS improves final accuracy by +2.71 percentage points over random and reduces average forgetting by about 33\% (0.0746 to 0.0496). Interference-only achieves slightly lower forgetting but does not surpass DyCLOS in \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}}\); similarity-only helps compared to random yet underperforms the dual-signal combination. These ablations confirm that both signals contribute to a favorable stability-plasticity trade-off.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Vision class-incremental quick run}
\begin{itemize}
  \item DyCLOS: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.1310\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0186\); chosen order .
  \item Random fixed order: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.1221\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.1591\); order .
  \item Similarity-only: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.0976\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0010\); order .
\end{itemize}
Interpretation. Despite low absolute accuracies typical of tiny backbones and few epochs, DyCLOS improves \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}}\) by 0.89 points and reduces forgetting by \(\approx 88\%\) (0.1591 to 0.0186). Similarity-only nearly eliminates forgetting but at a marked accuracy cost, highlighting the need to balance data similarity with gradient interference.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Synthetic NLP multi-task stream}
\begin{itemize}
  \item DyCLOS: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.7620\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0100\); order .
  \item Random fixed order: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.7290\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0170\); order .
  \item Similarity-only: \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.7150\); \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0000\); order .
\end{itemize}
Interpretation. DyCLOS improves \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}}\) by +3.3 points over random and reduces forgetting by \(\approx 41\%\) (0.0170 to 0.0100). Similarity-only again appears over-conservative: lowest forgetting but lowest accuracy.

\subsection{Additional quick run (degenerate case)}
A minimal follow-up run with four arriving tasks reported identical \(\Omega_{\mathrm{acc}} = 0.1500\) and \(\Omega_{\mathrm{forg}} = 0.0000\) for both DyCLOS and the fixed-order baseline, with probe and scheduling time of 3.59 s and only two tasks committed in the reported order. This non-discriminative run does not contradict the main findings but illustrates that ultra-short runs can mask scheduling benefits.

\subsection{Fairness, hyperparameters, and limitations}
Across runs, the adapter recipe and scheduler settings are held constant across baselines and ablations. The reported results use single seeds and prioritize directional effects over statistical significance. Transfer-Gain and confidence intervals are part of our broader plan but are absent from the compact logs. Absolute accuracies in Experiment 2 are low due to compact models and short training; nevertheless, forgetting reductions are substantial. Longer runs will better reflect realistic overheads, where the probe cost becomes negligible relative to training.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented DyCLOS, a dynamic curriculum for continual learning that is model-agnostic and scalable. DyCLOS combines a training-free dataset distance based on optimal transport with a gradient interference estimate from compact adapter probes to assign costs to task transitions and schedules tasks online via a sliding-window beam search. Training is confined to parameter-efficient adapters inspired by APD, and a stability-aware controller mitigates transient drops \cite{yoon-2019-scalable,lange-2022-continual,alvarezmelis-2020-geometric}.

Across three compact evaluations-synthetic vision, class-incremental vision, and synthetic NLP-DyCLOS improves final average accuracy by 0.9-3.3 percentage points over fixed or random orders and reduces average forgetting by 33-88\%, with small absolute overhead. Ablations confirm that using both similarity and interference signals yields the best stability-plasticity trade-off, consistent with evidence on the roles of task similarity and gradient alignment \cite{riemer-2018-learning,ramasesh-2020-anatomy,lee-2021-continual,guha-2024-the}.

Future work will extend evaluation to longer streams and larger backbones, report multi-seed confidence intervals and Transfer-Gain as in modular continual benchmarks \cite{veniat-2020-efficient}, and incorporate curriculum constraints for privacy or fairness \cite{bu-2024-pre}. We also plan to jointly optimize ordering and adapter capacity \cite{wang-2023-task,zhang-2023-overcoming} and to explore interactions with mode connectivity \cite{mirzadeh-2020-linear}. By elevating task ordering to a first-class decision, DyCLOS converts a source of variance into a controllable lever, enabling more robust and efficient continual pretraining pipelines.
