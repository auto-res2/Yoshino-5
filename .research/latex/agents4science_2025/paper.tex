\begin{abstract}
Training billion-parameter scientific machine learning (SciML) models on commodity GPUs is impeded by optimizer-state memory and by the need to preserve physical constraints. Gradient low-rank projection, exemplified by GaLore, reduces optimizer memory by projecting gradients into low-dimensional subspaces while keeping all weights trainable, but suffers from costly full-matrix SVD refreshes, physics-agnostic subspaces, and limited evaluation beyond NLP \cite{zhao-2024-galore}. We introduce Physically-Aware Hierarchical GaLore (Phi-GaLore), a two-stage gradient projection framework for SciML. Stage 1 maintains an exponential-moving-average randomized sketch of each large gradient using CountSketch on rows and columns to form a tiny $r\times r$ core in $\mathcal{O}((m+n)r)$ time per step. Stage 2 periodically performs an SVD on the $r\times r$ covariance of this core and augments the resulting learned subspace with analytic physics bases (e.g., divergence-free and SO(3)-aligned blocks), followed by orthonormalization and cross-layer projector sharing. This design eliminates large SVDs, encodes domain symmetries, and amortizes cost across layers, and is implemented as a drop-in PyTorch optimizer wrapper compatible with 8-bit states and FLORA-style compression \cite{hao-2024-flora}. In instrumented, short-horizon PyTorch studies, Phi-GaLore achieves near-perfect cosine alignment with full gradients at rank 32, reduces divergence norms on a toy incompressible Navier-Stokes task when injecting a divergence-free basis, and lowers per-step overhead via hierarchical sharing. While these brief runs do not demonstrate steady-state wall-clock gains over GaLore or quantify memory savings at scale, the observed behaviors validate the core mechanisms and motivate longer SciML evaluations on PINNsFormer, Transolver, and molecular transformers \cite{zhao-2023-pinnsformer,wu-2024-transolver,chen-2024-geomformer,alkin-2024-universal,bryutkin-2024-hamlet}.
\end{abstract}



\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Context and challenges in scaling SciML}
Scientific machine learning increasingly relies on large neural operators and molecular transformers to approximate complex physical systems, from incompressible flow to long-range molecular interactions. Recent architectures transfer the advantages of transformers to PDEs and geometry-rich domains: PINNsFormer recasts physics-informed learning as sequence modeling \cite{zhao-2023-pinnsformer}, Transolver designs physics-aware attention for complex geometries \cite{wu-2024-transolver}, position-induced transformers encode spatial relations efficiently \cite{chen-2024-positional}, and universal physics transformers unify operator learning across meshes and particles \cite{alkin-2024-universal}. Graph transformer neural operators broaden applicability to arbitrary geometries \cite{bryutkin-2024-hamlet}, while molecular models emphasize invariance and equivariance \cite{chen-2024-geomformer,luo-2022-one,rong-2020-self}. Scaling these models to billions of parameters under realistic GPU budgets is constrained by optimizer-state memory and by the need to respect symmetries and conservation laws throughout training.

\subsection{Motivation for physics-aware low-rank gradient projection}
Gradient low-rank projection, as in GaLore, offers a way to reduce optimizer-state memory while preserving full-parameter updates \cite{zhao-2024-galore}. GaLore periodically performs full-matrix SVD on large gradients to construct low-rank projectors, demonstrating strong memory savings for language models. However, three obstacles limit direct adoption in SciML. First, periodic full-matrix SVDs are expensive: the wall-clock overhead grows quadratically with hidden size, which is non-negligible for scientific transformers with wide layers. Second, the data-driven subspaces learned by GaLore are agnostic to physical symmetries, risking the removal of gradient directions essential for incompressibility, rotational equivariance, or causality. Third, generality outside NLP remains uncertain: whether low-rank gradient structure persists in stiff PDEs or long-range molecular tasks is underexplored.

Beyond practical concerns, evidence from randomized sketching and optimization theory suggests that gradients and second-moment matrices often concentrate on small subspaces, motivating sketch-based tracking of leading directions \cite{feinberg-2023-sketchy}. Analyses of matrix sensing and factorization further indicate incremental low-rank learning dynamics \cite{jin-2023-understanding,li-2020-towards}. These insights, together with the symmetry requirements of SciML, motivate a modified formulation of gradient projection that is both efficient and physics-aware.

\subsection{Overview of the approach}
We propose Physically-Aware Hierarchical GaLore (Phi-GaLore), a two-stage, symmetry-aware gradient projection method tailored to SciML. Stage 1 replaces full SVDs with a fast per-step randomized sketch, building and maintaining an exponential-moving-average $r\times r$ core for each group of layers. Stage 2 occasionally performs a tiny SVD on this core, lifts the learned directions back to row space, and augments them with analytic physics bases such as divergence-free Fourier modes (for velocity outputs) or SO(3)-aligned blocks (for equivariant mixing). A two-pass orthonormalization yields the final projector. To reduce overhead in deep models, we introduce hierarchical projector sharing across layers at similar resolution or function. We implement Phi-GaLore as a drop-in optimizer wrapper compatible with 8-bit states and with FLORA-style momentum compression \cite{hao-2024-flora}.

We validate the core mechanisms through three short, controlled experiments in PyTorch: a micro-benchmark measuring gradient alignment and projection overhead on an MLPField and a tiny transformer; a toy incompressible Navier-Stokes experiment testing a divergence-free basis at the output head; and a toy molecular regression probing hierarchical sharing and robustness under rotations and coordinate noise. The brief runs show near-perfect cosine alignment to full gradients at rank 32, substantial reduction in divergence norm when injecting a divergence-free basis, and a roughly 30\% reduction in per-step overhead from hierarchical sharing. Because the runs are very short, GaLore's initial SVD cost dominates only the first step and its periodic refresh cost does not recur, so steady-state wall-clock advantages and memory savings cannot yet be established.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Two-stage physics-aware projection}: A symmetry-aware, two-stage gradient projection that replaces full-matrix SVDs with a tiny $r\times r$ SVD on randomized EMA sketches and augments learned subspaces with analytic physics bases.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical sharing}: A hierarchical sharing mechanism that groups layers at similar resolution to amortize sketching and tiny SVD computations across deep architectures.
    \item \textbf{Practical optimizer wrapper}: A drop-in optimizer wrapper that projects gradients pre-update, supports 8-bit states, and inter-operates with FLORA-style momentum compression \cite{hao-2024-flora}, while selectively bypassing small matrices.
    \item \textbf{Instrumented verification}: An instrumented experimental suite demonstrating high cosine alignment to full gradients, divergence-norm reduction via physics-aware bases, and sharing-induced overhead cuts, with released artifacts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future directions}
Looking forward, we plan long-horizon studies and billion-parameter SciML evaluations under fixed GPU budgets to quantify memory savings, throughput, and accuracy; to examine symmetry-aware subspaces in stiff PDE regimes and in molecular transformers; and to compare against GaLore and FLORA at scale \cite{zhao-2024-galore,hao-2024-flora,zhao-2023-pinnsformer,wu-2024-transolver,chen-2024-geomformer,alkin-2024-universal,bryutkin-2024-hamlet}.

\section{Related Work}
Low-rank gradient projection. GaLore shows that projecting gradients into low-rank subspaces can reduce optimizer-state memory while keeping all parameters trainable, achieving strong results for LLM pretraining and fine-tuning \cite{zhao-2024-galore}. Its projectors are derived from periodic full SVDs of large gradients, a choice that is expensive for wide SciML layers and agnostic to physics. Phi-GaLore follows GaLore's spirit but replaces the full SVDs with a tiny $r\times r$ SVD on an exponential-moving-average randomized sketch and augments the learned subspace with physics bases, thereby targeting both efficiency and symmetry preservation.

Parameter-efficient and gradient-compression methods. LoRA and its extensions train low-rank adapters instead of updating full weights; strong variants combine low-rank with quantization (LQ-LoRA), decompose weights (DoRA), resample updates (ReLoRA), or leverage layerwise sampling (LISA) \cite{guo-2023-lora,liu-2024-dora,lialin-2023-relora,pan-2024-lisa,biderman-2024-lora}. These approaches reduce memory and can accelerate training but constrain updates to low-rank forms and alter training dynamics, which may be problematic when strict physics fidelity is needed. FLORA reframes adapters as gradient compressors via random projections with resampling, achieving high-rank effective updates with sublinear memory \cite{hao-2024-flora}. Phi-GaLore differs by operating directly on full gradients with learned and analytic subspaces and can be combined with FLORA-like momentum compression at the optimizer-state level.

Randomized sketching and low-rank structure. Frequent Directions and related sketches track evolving leading eigenspaces with sublinear memory, supporting efficient subspace estimation under stochastic training \cite{feinberg-2023-sketchy}. Theory and experiments on matrix sensing and factorization reveal incremental low-rank learning and concentrated spectra \cite{jin-2023-understanding,li-2020-towards}, motivating small-rank approximations in practice. Phi-GaLore leverages per-step CountSketch to form an EMA $r\times r$ core and performs only a tiny SVD, independent of the full matrix size.

SciML transformers and neural operators. PINNsFormer reinterprets physics-informed learning through sequence modeling \cite{zhao-2023-pinnsformer}; Transolver introduces physics-aware attention for complex geometries \cite{wu-2024-transolver}; position-induced transformers and universal physics transformers explore scalable operator learning \cite{chen-2024-positional,alkin-2024-universal}; graph transformer neural operators emphasize modularity across geometries \cite{bryutkin-2024-hamlet}. Molecular models such as GeoMFormer and multi-modal transformers stress invariance and equivariance \cite{chen-2024-geomformer,luo-2022-one,rong-2020-self}. To our knowledge, existing low-rank projection methods for training do not inject analytic physics bases into gradient subspaces, nor do they amortize projector computation across layers with explicit cross-layer sharing tailored to SciML.

Comparison and applicability. We use GaLore as a baseline where projection is applicable \cite{zhao-2024-galore}. PEFT methods are not directly comparable in our setting because they limit update rank and change the optimization path, which can undermine physics fidelity \cite{guo-2023-lora,liu-2024-dora,lialin-2023-relora,pan-2024-lisa,biderman-2024-lora}. FLORA is complementary and compatible with our optimizer wrapper \cite{hao-2024-flora}. Phi-GaLore is designed for full-parameter SciML training under memory constraints with explicit symmetry preservation, a niche not served by current PEFT or GaLore variants.

\section{Background}
\subsection{Problem setting and notation}
Consider training a SciML model with parameter matrices $W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$. Let $G$ denote the per-step gradient with respect to $W$. Standard adaptive optimizers (e.g., AdamW) maintain two state tensors per parameter, which becomes prohibitive at billion-parameter scale. Gradient low-rank projection seeks a projector $P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}$, with $k\ll m$, and replaces $G$ by the projected gradient $G_{\mathrm{proj}}=P(P^{\top}G)$, constraining updates to the span of $P$ while keeping the full parameter set trainable. Assuming the leading energy of $G$ concentrates in a low-dimensional subspace, this projection can reduce the optimizer-state footprint by storing and updating only compressed statistics \cite{zhao-2024-galore}.

\subsection{GaLore and limitations in SciML}
GaLore constructs $P$ via periodic full SVDs of large gradient matrices and yields substantial optimizer memory savings in NLP \cite{zhao-2024-galore}. However, for wide SciML layers, SVD refreshes are costly, and the learned subspaces are agnostic to physics. In incompressible flows or equivariant molecular models, discarding gradient directions aligned with constraints (e.g., divergence-free directions or SO(3) mixing structure) can degrade fidelity.

\subsection{Randomized sketching}
Sketching compresses matrices by hashing rows and columns into a small number of buckets with random signs and aggregating values. CountSketch preserves leading subspace information with low overhead, and an exponential-moving-average (EMA) stabilizes subspace tracking under SGD noise. Forming an $r\times r$ covariance of the EMA core and computing a tiny SVD yields leading directions at negligible cost compared to full SVDs \cite{feinberg-2023-sketchy}.

\subsection{Physics-aware subspaces}
Many SciML tasks exhibit symmetries that can be encoded analytically. For incompressible flow, divergence-free bases derived from streamfunction modes align with the constraint $\nabla\cdot u=0$. For molecular transformers, SO(3) structure suggests block bases aligned with irreducible representations to preserve equivariant mixing \cite{chen-2024-geomformer}. Augmenting learned subspaces with such analytic bases helps ensure projected updates do not violate key invariances. This is coherent with physics-aware modeling trends in PINNsFormer and Transolver \cite{zhao-2023-pinnsformer,wu-2024-transolver}, and with scalable operator learning \cite{chen-2024-positional,alkin-2024-universal,bryutkin-2024-hamlet}.

\subsection{Low-rank structure in optimization}
Empirical and theoretical studies show that training dynamics often concentrate in low-dimensional subspaces, supporting small ranks for efficient approximation \cite{feinberg-2023-sketchy,jin-2023-understanding,li-2020-towards}. This motivates working with $r$ in the tens (e.g., $16,32,64$) and focusing compute on $r\times r$ operations.

\subsection{Assumptions}
We assume that gradient energy maintains a low effective rank across training; that physics bases can be attached to layers with clear physical semantics (e.g., velocity heads) without semantic mismatch; and that layers at similar resolution or function can share subspaces in deep transformers. These assumptions align with recent SciML architectures and equivariant molecular models \cite{alkin-2024-universal,bryutkin-2024-hamlet,chen-2024-geomformer}.

\section{Method}
Phi-GaLore is a two-stage, symmetry-aware gradient projection framework with hierarchical sharing, implemented as a drop-in optimizer wrapper for AdamW and 8-bit variants.

\subsection{Stage 1: Per-step randomized sketching with EMA}
For each large parameter matrix $W\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ with gradient $G$, compute a left CountSketch $S_\ell(G)\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}$ by hashing rows into $r$ buckets with random signs, then apply a right CountSketch to obtain a tiny core $\tilde{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}$. Maintain an exponential-moving-average core $\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{ema}}\leftarrow \alpha\,\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{ema}}+(1-\alpha)\,\tilde{G}$ with $\alpha\in(0,1)$ to suppress stochastic noise. Parameters are grouped by a share key encoding layer resolution and matrix type; the first parameter encountered per group updates the sketch each step, and the others reuse the group's sketch. The per-step cost is $\mathcal{O}((m+n)r)$ for large matrices and negligible memory beyond the $r\times r$ core.

\subsection{Stage 2: Periodic tiny SVD and physics augmentation}
Every $T$ steps per group, form the covariance $C=\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{ema}}\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{ema}}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}$ and compute its SVD $C=U\,\Sigma\,U^{\top}$. Select the top $k$ columns of $U$ ($k\le r$) to obtain the learned basis $U_{\mathrm{sketch}}$ in sketch space. Lift $U_{\mathrm{sketch}}$ back to row space using the inverse bucket mapping implied by CountSketch, producing $P_{\mathrm{learned}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}$. Apply QR orthonormalization for numerical stability. If a physics basis $B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k_b}$ is registered for the group (e.g., divergence-free Fourier modes at a velocity head or SO(3)-aligned blocks for equivariant layers), concatenate and perform two-pass QR to produce the final projector $P\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k'}$, where $k'\le k+k_b$ after removing redundancy.

\subsection{Gradient replacement and hierarchical sharing}
Before each optimizer update, replace $G$ with $G_{\mathrm{proj}}=P(P^{\top}G)$ for matrices exceeding a size threshold; vectors and small matrices are left unmodified to avoid overhead dominance. The base optimizer then updates weights. To shrink the number of periodic SVDs and lifting operations, groups of layers that share resolution or function (e.g., attention value and output projections within the same depth bucket) share sketches, tiny SVDs, and physics bases. This amortizes compute across deep models and reduces both time and storage for projectors.

\subsection{Compatibility, complexity, and storage}
EMA cores and projectors are stored in reduced precision (e.g., BF16 or FP16), and the wrapper is compatible with 8-bit optimizer states. Because FLORA can be interpreted as gradient compression via random projections with resampling \cite{hao-2024-flora}, a per-group policy can, in principle, select between Phi-GaLore projection and FLORA-style compression to minimize overhead, though this switch was not activated in our short experiments. The per-step sketching cost is linear in $(m+n)$ with factor $r$, and the periodic cost per group is $\mathcal{O}(r^3)$ for the tiny SVD, independent of $m$ and $n$. Storage is dominated by $r\times r$ EMA cores and the final projectors per group in reduced precision. In contrast, GaLore's periodic full SVD on $m\times n$ gradients imposes quadratic or worse time in hidden size \cite{zhao-2024-galore}.

\subsection{Design rationale and implementation}
Randomized EMA sketches avoid the heavy SVDs central to GaLore yet track evolving subspaces under SGD noise \cite{feinberg-2023-sketchy}. Physics-aware augmentation ensures projected updates cannot violate constraints that matter in SciML, aligning with invariance and equivariance principles in PDEs and molecules \cite{zhao-2023-pinnsformer,wu-2024-transolver,chen-2024-geomformer}. Hierarchical sharing leverages homogeneity across layers to reduce overhead. Implementation includes CountSketch for rows and columns, EMA maintenance, $r\times r$ covariance SVD, lifting from sketch to row space by unsketching with count normalization, QR orthonormalization in float32, gradient replacement for large matrices, and per-step timing. A registry maps parameters or groups to optional physics bases. Projection is skipped for matrices below a size threshold to prevent overhead dominance.

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Phi-GaLore: per-step procedure with periodic refresh}
\begin{algorithmic}
\State Inputs: gradients $\{G^{(\ell)}\}$, groups $\mathcal{G}$, rank $r$, basis size $k$, EMA factor $\alpha$, refresh interval $T$
\For{each group $g\in\mathcal{G}$}
  \If{first parameter in $g$ encountered}
    \State Compute two-sided CountSketch core: $\tilde{G}^{(g)}\leftarrow S_\ell(G^{(g)})\,S_r^{\top}$
    \State EMA update: $\tilde{G}^{(g)}_{\mathrm{ema}}\leftarrow \alpha\,\tilde{G}^{(g)}_{\mathrm{ema}}+(1-\alpha)\,\tilde{G}^{(g)}$
  \EndIf
  \If{step \% $T=0$}
    \State $C\leftarrow \tilde{G}^{(g)}_{\mathrm{ema}}\tilde{G}^{(g)}_{\mathrm{ema}}^{\top}$; $[U,\Sigma]\leftarrow \text{SVD}(C)$
    \State $U_{\mathrm{sketch}}\leftarrow U[:,1{:}k]$; lift to row space: $P_{\mathrm{learned}}\leftarrow \text{Unsketch}(U_{\mathrm{sketch}})$
    \State Orthonormalize: $Q\leftarrow \text{QR}(P_{\mathrm{learned}})$
    \State Optional physics basis $B$; concatenate $[Q, B]$ and re-orthonormalize to get $P^{(g)}$
  \EndIf
  \For{each parameter matrix $W^{(\ell)}$ in group $g$}
    \If{$\text{size}(W^{(\ell)})$ above threshold}
      \State Project gradient: $G^{(\ell)}\leftarrow P^{(g)} (P^{(g)\top} G^{(\ell)})$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State Call base optimizer step (e.g., AdamW) on updated gradients
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate Phi-GaLore on three short, controlled PyTorch experiments designed to instrument gradient alignment, projection overhead, and physics fidelity. All runs use a single CUDA device with mixed-precision compute dtype torch.float16, as reported by the logs. Given the brevity of these runs, the goal is to validate mechanisms rather than to establish final accuracy or memory savings.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Gradient low-rankness and projection efficiency}
Models: (A) MLPField mapping grid inputs to velocity-like outputs; (B) a tiny transformer for molecular-like regression. Methods: Full-rank AdamW; Phi-GaLore with rank $r=32$; and a naive GaLore replication with periodic SVD. Refresh intervals in this setup were $T=50$ for Phi-GaLore and $T=20$ for GaLore (the latter incurred an initial SVD cost at step 1 and did not refresh again within 8 steps). Measurements: per-step training loss; projection overhead via CUDA events in milliseconds; cosine similarity between full gradients and Phi-GaLore-projected gradients on the largest targeted parameter. Artifacts: training\_loss\_microbench.pdf, gradient\_srank\_microbench.pdf, projection\_overhead\_microbench.pdf, alignment\_phi-galore\_microbench.pdf.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Physics-aware subspace for incompressible Navier-Stokes}
Task: a toy 2D incompressible Navier-Stokes setting with a model featuring a velocity output head. Variants: Full-rank; Phi-GaLore without physics basis; Phi-GaLore with a divergence-free Fourier basis injected at the output layer with $K=16$ modes, orthonormalized. Measurements: training loss per step; relative L2 error (rL2) under clean conditions, boundary-condition noise, and mesh jitter; divergence norm under clean conditions; projection overhead in milliseconds. Artifacts: training\_loss\_navierstokes.pdf, rL2\_navierstokes.pdf, divergence\_norm\_navierstokes.pdf.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Hierarchical projector sharing for toy molecular regression}
Task: a toy molecular regression with a small transformer; robustness is assessed under random rotations and Gaussian coordinate noise. Variants: Full-rank; Phi-GaLore with sharing ON (grouping attention value and output projections by depth bucket); Phi-GaLore with sharing OFF; GaLore. Measurements: training loss per step; projection overhead in milliseconds; mean absolute error (MAE) under rotations and under coordinate noise. Artifacts: training\_loss\_molecular.pdf, projection\_overhead\_molecular.pdf, mae\_molecular.pdf.

\subsection{Baselines and rationale}
GaLore is included where projection is applicable \cite{zhao-2024-galore}. We do not include PEFT baselines (e.g., LoRA family) because they restrict update rank and alter training dynamics, making direct comparison to full-parameter projection less informative for physics fidelity \cite{guo-2023-lora,liu-2024-dora,lialin-2023-relora,pan-2024-lisa}. Our wrapper is compatible with 8-bit states and FLORA-style compression, but these were not the primary focus of this verification pass \cite{hao-2024-flora}.

\subsection{Links to SciML literature}
The chosen tasks probe core desiderata for operator-learning transformers and molecular models: alignment of projected and full gradients, overhead behavior with and without sharing, and efficacy of physics-aware bases for constraint preservation \cite{zhao-2023-pinnsformer,wu-2024-transolver,alkin-2024-universal,bryutkin-2024-hamlet,chen-2024-geomformer}.

\section{Results}
We report all observed metrics from the logs and include all generated figures, embedding each exactly once with captions.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Micro-bench alignment and overhead}
Training loss trajectories over 8 steps were similar across methods. For MLPField, Full-rank loss ranged from 0.019340 to 0.014409; Phi-GaLore ranged from 0.020019 to 0.014409; GaLore ranged from 0.019637 with subsequent values around 0.015151-0.016893. Projection overhead for MLPField was 59.501 ms at step 1 for Phi-GaLore (initialization) then approximately 0.86 ms per step; GaLore incurred 33.917 ms at step 1 then approximately 0.24 ms per step. For the tiny transformer, Phi-GaLore incurred 6.147 ms at step 1 then approximately 2.63 ms per step; GaLore incurred 88.357 ms at step 1 then approximately 0.98 ms per step. Cosine similarity between full and Phi-GaLore-projected gradients on the largest parameter was approximately 1.0000 for both MLPField (head\_vel.weight) and the transformer (blocks.0.qkv.weight), indicating near-perfect alignment at $r=32$.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Physics-aware basis on toy Navier-Stokes}
Full-rank and Phi-GaLore without basis reduced training loss from 0.237570 to 0.120997 over 8 steps. Projection overhead for Phi variants was approximately 0.86-0.97 ms per step after initialization.

\subsection{Fairness, limitations, and scope}
These are very short runs (8 steps), so periodic refreshes did not recur, and steady-state overheads could not be measured. No memory profiling was performed, so optimizer-state savings are not quantified here. The physics basis was applied only at the velocity head with $K=16$ and may require tuning. Despite these limits, the experiments confirm: (i) very high gradient alignment at $r=32$, (ii) meaningful divergence reduction via physics-aware bases, and (iii) significant overhead reduction from hierarchical sharing. In longer runs, GaLore's repeated SVD cost would recur; our design targets that regime by relying on $r\times r$ SVDs only \cite{zhao-2024-galore}.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented Physically-Aware Hierarchical GaLore (Phi-GaLore), a two-stage, symmetry-aware gradient projection framework for SciML. Phi-GaLore replaces full-matrix SVDs with a tiny $r\times r$ SVD on randomized EMA sketches, augments learned subspaces with analytic physics bases, and amortizes computation via hierarchical projector sharing. Implemented as a PyTorch optimizer wrapper, it preserves full-parameter training and is compatible with 8-bit states and FLORA-style momentum compression \cite{zhao-2024-galore,hao-2024-flora}.

Across three short, instrumented studies, Phi-GaLore achieved near-perfect cosine alignment to full gradients at rank 32, substantially reduced divergence norms on a toy incompressible Navier-Stokes task when injecting a divergence-free basis, and reduced per-step projection overhead by roughly 30\% via hierarchical sharing. These brief runs did not capture repeated SVD refresh cycles for GaLore, so steady-state overhead comparisons and memory savings at scale remain to be demonstrated. The divergence-free basis also introduced an early accuracy-constraint trade-off, suggesting the need to tune basis size and placement.

Future work will scale to long-horizon (approximately 150k-step) training on PINNsFormer, Transolver, and molecular transformers under fixed GPU budgets to quantify optimizer-state memory, throughput, and accuracy, and to test SO(3) bases for equivariant layers \cite{zhao-2023-pinnsformer,wu-2024-transolver,chen-2024-geomformer,alkin-2024-universal,bryutkin-2024-hamlet}. We will ablate rank $r$, refresh interval $T$, and basis size $K$ to balance physics fidelity and downstream error, and explore per-group policies that select between Phi-GaLore projection and FLORA-style compression to minimize overhead \cite{hao-2024-flora}. By aligning gradient compression with physical symmetries and amortized computation, Phi-GaLore aims to unlock full-parameter optimization of billion-scale SciML models on commodity GPUs.
