\begin{abstract}
Sparse attention, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and 4-bit quantization each improve the efficiency of small language models, yet jointly they often degrade exact multi-step mathematical reasoning despite near-identical perplexity. We address this with MAGMA, a magnitude-guided adaptive masked attention mechanism that learns block-sparse attention patterns during DoRA fine-tuning and reuses them at inference with zero per-sample overhead. MAGMA repurposes DoRAs learned per-row magnitude channel as a stable token-importance signal, combines it with Q/K activation norms into per-block scores, aggregates these via an exponential moving average, and solves a global budgeted selection to produce GPU-friendly masks that enable K/V compaction in Flash-style kernels. Unlike runtime-estimated or RL-based sparsity, MAGMA learns once, is fully compatible with 4-bit NF4 adaptation, and preserves training stability by decoupling mask statistics from main gradients. We release a PyTorch-first reference and evaluate three axes: efficacy under high sparsity, long-context robustness via mask tiling, and token-importance faithfulness. In our small-scale reference runs (10 steps, synthetic data, no optimized kernels), all models reach 0\% exact match; however, MAGMAs keep probabilities correlate with math-token density (Spearman 0.51), supporting the core design. We discuss limitations and a roadmap toward conclusive evaluation on 7B backbones with Flash-compatible sparse kernels and standardized math benchmarks \cite{liu-2024-dora,dettmers-2023-qlora,dettmers-2022-the,pagliardini-2023-fast,tay-2020-long}.
\end{abstract}



\section{Introduction}
Adapting large language models efficiently has converged on a combination of parameter-efficient fine-tuning, quantization, and optimized attention. LoRA reduces trainable parameters while preserving quality, and DoRA further decomposes pretrained weights into magnitude and direction, narrowing the gap to full fine-tuning without inference overhead \cite{liu-2024-dora}. In parallel, quantization has matured: QLoRA enables 4-bit NF4 adaptation with minimal accuracy loss and practical memory footprints \cite{dettmers-2023-qlora}, while scaling analyses suggest 4-bit is often optimal per bit budget \cite{dettmers-2022-the}. Additional advances such as LLM.int8() and SmoothQuant mitigate outliers and activation quantization challenges \cite{dettmers-2022-bit,xiao-2022-smoothquant}. On the attention side, dynamic-sparse FlashAttention provides a route to \(O(n\cdot k)\) complexity with minimal overhead \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}, and long-sequence benchmarks clarify evaluation protocols \cite{tay-2020-long}.

Yet when these ingredients are combined - PEFT (LoRA/DoRA), 4-bit quantization, and sparse attention - practitioners report a recurring failure case on exact multi-step mathematical reasoning: accuracy drops by several points on GSM8K and MATH even when perplexity remains unchanged. Our hypothesis is that task-agnostic sparse patterns preferentially prune intermediate scratch-pad tokens (digits, delimiters, step markers) that are disproportionately important for algebraic reasoning, thereby harming execution correctness while leaving language modeling perplexity intact. Prior work on sparse or linear attention often employs fixed patterns, per-sample mask estimation, or end-to-end learned sparsity that introduces runtime or stability costs \cite{li-2020-sac,lee-2023-sea,chen-2021-scatterbrain}.

We introduce MAGMA (Magnitude-guided Adaptive Masked Attention), a plug-in module that learns attention sparsity patterns during DoRA-based fine-tuning and reuses them at inference with zero per-sample computation. The key idea is to repurpose DoRAs learned magnitude vectors as low-variance importance priors: combined with per-token Q/K activation norms, they yield per-block scores aggregated with an exponential moving average (EMA). A global allocator then selects the highest-scoring blocks across layers and heads under a target sparsity, producing block-aligned masks amenable to K/V compaction in Flash-style attention \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}. MAGMA therefore unifies PEFT and efficient attention under heavy quantization while avoiding online estimation or reinforcement learning.

This problem is challenging for three reasons. First, naive sparsity tends to remove precisely the tokens that anchor arithmetic invariants. Second, learning masks online can destabilize optimization, especially when combined with quantization. Third, dynamic sparsity often underperforms dense FlashAttention unless masks are precompiled and block-structured to favor contiguous memory movement \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}. MAGMA addresses these issues by using (i) DoRA magnitudes as stable, learned signals, (ii) EMA to amortize noisy activation statistics across training steps, and (iii) frozen, block-aligned masks that are GPU-friendly and introduce no runtime learning cost.

We provide a reproducible PyTorch reference and evaluate three aspects in a deliberately minimal setup: math reasoning efficacy under high sparsity, long-context mask tiling, and token-importance faithfulness. Because we train small models from scratch for only \(\approx 60\) steps and do not integrate optimized kernels, exact-match (EM) accuracy remains at 0\% across conditions and dense attention appears faster due to Python overhead. Nevertheless, MAGMAs masks correlate positively with math-token density, supporting the central intuition.

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Magnitude-guided masks:} A magnitude-guided mask learning mechanism that integrates with DoRA and 4-bit NF4 adaptation to produce reusable block-sparse attention patterns with \(O(n\cdot k)\) behavior via K/V compaction \cite{liu-2024-dora,dettmers-2023-qlora,dettmers-2022-the,pagliardini-2023-fast}.
  \item \textbf{Global allocator:} A lightweight global allocator over EMA-aggregated per-block scores that yields fixed binary masks satisfying GPU constraints, removing per-sample computation \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}.
  \item \textbf{Reproducible pipeline:} A reproducible pipeline with ablations and diagnostics, including a token-importance faithfulness analysis showing positive correlation between mask keep probability and math-token density.
  \item \textbf{Limitations and roadmap:} A limitations analysis and roadmap toward conclusive evaluation on GSM8K and MATH with pretrained 7B backbones and Flash-compatible sparse kernels; we situate MAGMA among dynamic sparse and hybrid methods \cite{lee-2023-sea,chen-2021-scatterbrain} and discuss compatibility with KV-cache quantization for extreme contexts \cite{hooper-2024-kvquant}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Broader context and impact}
MAGMA complements work on PEFT (including VeRA and deep low-rank refinements) \cite{kopiczko-2023-vera,yaras-2024-compressible}, the theory of low-rank expressivity \cite{zeng-2023-the}, and specialized or tool-integrated approaches to mathematical reasoning \cite{fu-2023-specializing,yu-2023-metamath,gou-2023-tora}. Quantization behavior at scale highlights the need for optimization-aware designs \cite{ahmadian-2023-intriguing}. Multi-modal evaluations further reveal persistent reasoning gaps \cite{lu-2023-mathvista,cherian-2024-evaluating}, motivating methods that retain reasoning-critical content under tight efficiency budgets.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Parameter-efficient fine-tuning and weight decompositions}
LoRA reduces adaptation cost by injecting low-rank updates into frozen backbones, while DoRA decomposes weights into magnitude and direction, enabling improved learning capacity without inference overhead \cite{liu-2024-dora}. VeRA shares a single low-rank pair across layers with per-layer scaling vectors \cite{kopiczko-2023-vera}, and deep low-rank methods refine the dynamics and regularization of LoRA-style updates \cite{yaras-2024-compressible}. MAGMA is orthogonal to these methods: rather than improving the adaptation space alone, it exploits DoRAs learned magnitude channel as a stable importance signal to drive attention sparsity.

\subsection{Quantization for efficient adaptation}
QLoRA popularized 4-bit NF4 adaptation with double quantization, enabling economical fine-tuning while preserving performance \cite{dettmers-2023-qlora}. Scaling analyses argue that 4-bit precision is often optimal for a given bit budget \cite{dettmers-2022-the}. LLM.int8() isolates outliers to mixed-precision paths, and SmoothQuant migrates activation quantization difficulty into weights, stabilizing W8A8 inference \cite{dettmers-2022-bit,xiao-2022-smoothquant}. MAGMA is designed to be compatible with such low-precision regimes, relying on stable magnitude signals and blockwise patterns that are robust to quantization.

\subsection{Sparse and hybrid attention}
Scatterbrain unifies sparse and low-rank approximations and shows benefits depend on softmax temperature regimes \cite{chen-2021-scatterbrain}. SEA estimates attention via kernel-based linear attention and top-k selection, performing sparse attention at runtime \cite{lee-2023-sea}. SAC learns sparse attention graphs end-to-end \cite{li-2020-sac}. Dynamic sparse FlashAttention extends FlashAttention to key/query dropping and hashing with minimal overhead \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}. MAGMA differs by learning a reusable, task-aware mask once during training using EMA-aggregated statistics and DoRA magnitudes, eliminating runtime estimation and avoiding additional parameters or control flow.

\subsection{Reasoning-oriented adaptation and evaluation}
Specialized training can distill multi-step reasoning into smaller models \cite{fu-2023-specializing,yu-2023-metamath}, and tool-integrated agents delegate computation for harder math problems \cite{gou-2023-tora}. These methods rely on preserving intermediate reasoning content. MAGMA targets the infrastructure side: ensuring sparse attention retains math-critical tokens that such strategies need. Long-context evaluations via LRA and advances in FlashAttention encourage focusing on realistic sequence lengths \cite{tay-2020-long,pagliardini-2023-fast}. KVQuant shows KV-cache compression can push context lengths to extremes \cite{hooper-2024-kvquant}, a regime where learned sparsity may be particularly valuable.

\subsection{Contrastive summary}
Compared to SEA, MAGMA removes per-sample mask estimation and leverages a learned magnitude prior from DoRA \cite{lee-2023-sea,liu-2024-dora}. Compared to SAC, MAGMA avoids runtime graph construction and additional parameters \cite{li-2020-sac}. Scatterbrain aims to approximate dense attention via hybrid decompositions \cite{chen-2021-scatterbrain}, while MAGMA produces fixed, block-aligned masks intended for reuse in Flash-style kernels. Sparse Transformers can be universally expressive under suitable connectivity assumptions \cite{yun-2020-connections}, but the challenge is to achieve task-aware connectivity under strict GPU and quantization constraints - precisely MAGMAs focus.

\section{Background}
\subsection{Problem setting}
We consider a causal Transformer with L layers, H attention heads per layer, sequence length S, and head dimension D. Standard self-attention scales quadratically with S, becoming the primary bottleneck for long contexts \cite{tay-2020-long}. To reduce cost, we use blockwise key sparsity: the key sequence is partitioned into KB contiguous blocks of size B (KB equals \(\lceil S/B \rceil\)). For each layer l and head h, a binary mask \(M(l,h,b)\) indicates whether key block b is kept. Queries remain dense; keys and values are compacted according to M.

\subsection{DoRA magnitude channel as an importance prior}
DoRA decomposes each pretrained weight vector into a magnitude and a direction, learning a low-rank directional update while adapting magnitudes \cite{liu-2024-dora}. For the Q and K projection matrices, we compute per-head L2 norms of the learned magnitude vectors. These magnitudes tend to vary slowly and reflect stable channel importance accumulated during adaptation. MAGMA uses them as low-variance priors to complement noisy, step-level activation statistics.

\subsection{Quantization regime}
We assume 4-bit NF4 quantization of backbone weights with bfloat16 computation as in QLoRA \cite{dettmers-2023-qlora}. Prior work shows 4-bit is typically optimal in accuracy-per-bit trade-offs \cite{dettmers-2022-the}, while mixed-precision and activation smoothing address outlier and activation quantization challenges \cite{dettmers-2022-bit,xiao-2022-smoothquant}. Under such precision, mask stability and block alignment are essential for kernel efficiency \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}.

\subsection{Scoring and aggregation}
For each training step, we capture Q and K activations via forward hooks. For layer l, head h, and token t, let \(q(l,h,t)\) and \(k(l,h,t)\) be the corresponding vectors, with norms computed in floating point. For block b spanning tokens from \(bB\) to \((b+1)B-1\), we define an instantaneous block statistic as the mean of the product of Q and K norms over the block. We scale this by the product of the DoRA magnitude norms for the Q and K projections at \((l,h)\). We maintain an EMA table \(T(l,h,b)\) with decay 0.95 that aggregates these scaled scores over steps using no-gradient updates, decoupling mask learning from backpropagation.

\subsection{Budgeted allocation and constraints}
Given a target global drop rate \(\rho\), a global allocator selects the top \((1-\rho)\) fraction of blocks by T across all layers and heads, subject to a minimum of one kept block per head to avoid degenerate heads. Masks are recomputed every N steps and frozen between updates. We warm up sparsity from an initial value to \(\rho\) over early steps to avoid churn.

\subsection{Assumptions and theoretical context}
We assume access to DoRA magnitudes during training and block-aligned compaction at inference. Sparse Transformer theory indicates that, with appropriate connectivity, sparse attention can remain expressive \cite{yun-2020-connections}. MAGMA aims to satisfy GPU kernel and quantization constraints while biasing connectivity toward task-relevant tokens through learned statistics, rather than relying on fixed or per-sample dynamic patterns \cite{chen-2021-scatterbrain,lee-2023-sea,pagliardini-2023-fast}.

\section{Method}
MAGMA comprises four components integrated into DoRA-based fine-tuning under 4-bit quantization.

\subsection{Magnitude-guided block scoring}
We register forward hooks on the Q and K projections to capture activations during training. For each layer l, head h, and token t, we compute the L2 norms of \(q(l,h,t)\) and \(k(l,h,t)\). For each block b of size B, we take the mean over tokens in the block of the product of these norms, producing an activation-only score. We multiply this by the product of per-head DoRA magnitude norms for the Q and K projections at \((l,h)\), yielding \(s(l,h,b)\). This design leverages instantaneous activation strength while stabilizing it with a learned, low-variance magnitude prior \cite{liu-2024-dora}. We update an EMA table \(T(l,h,b)\) with decay factor 0.95 in no-grad mode to avoid interfering with gradient flow.

\subsection{Global budget allocator with sparsity warmup}
Given a target drop rate \(\rho\), we select \(K_{\text{keep}}=(1-\rho)\cdot(L\cdot H\cdot KB)\) blocks to maximize the retained total EMA score. Because the objective is separable and the budget is global, the optimum is obtained by selecting the \(K_{\text{keep}}\) indices with the largest \(T(l,h,b)\) over all layers and heads. We then enforce a per-head minimum of one kept block to avoid degenerate attention. Allocation is performed every N steps (default \(N=500\)). We warm up \(\rho\) from 0.7 to its target over the first training phase to ensure stable convergence. Masks are frozen between allocations to minimize churn and to match kernel expectations.

\subsection{Flash-compatible sparse attention via K/V compaction}
At inference and during training forward passes, the binary mask \(M(l,h,b)\) is used to compact keys and values per head by concatenating only tokens belonging to kept blocks. Queries remain length S. Attention is computed between dense queries and compacted keys/values, reducing per-head complexity from \(O(S\cdot S)\) to \(O(S\cdot k)\), where k is the number of kept key positions for that head. Our reference uses a correctness-first PyTorch path that performs softmax attention over compacted K/V. For production use, SCFA-style kernels support key dropping with minimal overhead beyond compaction \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}. Because masks are precomputed and block-aligned, there is no per-sample selection cost, and the operation is amenable to highly optimized fused kernels.

\subsection{Joint training with DoRA under 4-bit NF4}
We load a quantized backbone in 4-bit NF4 with bfloat16 computation \cite{dettmers-2023-qlora}. DoRA (rank 8) adapters are applied to attention and MLP projections \cite{liu-2024-dora}. Training uses AdamW with cosine decay, mixed precision, and gradient clipping. Mask statistics are updated in no-grad mode; masks themselves are not differentiated. We freeze masks over the final fraction of training to stabilize convergence.

\subsection{Ablations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Score components:} activation-only (remove magnitude scaling), magnitude-only (remove activation norms), and combined (default).
  \item \textbf{Allocator:} replace with uniform random block selection at the same sparsity.
  \item \textbf{Block size:} \(B \in \{16, 32\}\).
  \item \textbf{PEFT variant:} swap DoRA for LoRA to test the necessity of magnitude priors \cite{liu-2024-dora}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Complexity and memory}
With k kept keys per head, the attention complexity becomes \(O(S\cdot k)\). Because masks are block-aligned, memory movement during compaction is contiguous, favored by Flash-style kernels \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}. MAGMA introduces no runtime learning cost and no additional parameters beyond PEFT. The mask storage is negligible: a binary tensor of size \(L\times H\times KB\).

\subsection{Intended deployment and compatibility}
Learned masks are indexed by (layer, head) and reused for all inputs, preserving latency. MAGMA is compatible with KV-cache quantization and thus can be combined with techniques like KVQuant for extreme context lengths \cite{hooper-2024-kvquant}.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{MAGMA mask learning and allocation}
\begin{algorithmic}
  \State initialize EMA table \(T(l,h,b) \leftarrow 0\) for all layers l, heads h, blocks b
  \State set allocation interval N, EMA decay \(\gamma=0.95\), warmup schedule for \(\rho\)
  \For{each training step s}
    \State capture Q/K activations per layer/head/token via forward hooks
    \For{each layer l and head h}
      \For{each block b of size B}
        \State compute per-token norms \(\lVert q(l,h,t) \rVert_2\), \(\lVert k(l,h,t) \rVert_2\)
        \State \(u(l,h,b) \leftarrow \text{mean}_{t\in b}\big(\lVert q(l,h,t) \rVert_2\cdot \lVert k(l,h,t) \rVert_2\big)\)
        \State get DoRA magnitude norms \(m_q(l,h), m_k(l,h)\)
        \State \(s(l,h,b) \leftarrow u(l,h,b)\cdot m_q(l,h)\cdot m_k(l,h)\)
        \State update EMA: \(T(l,h,b) \leftarrow \gamma\,T(l,h,b) + (1-\gamma)\,s(l,h,b)\)
      \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \If{\(s \bmod N = 0\)}
      \State determine target drop rate \(\rho\) from warmup schedule
      \State set \(K_{\text{keep}}=(1-\rho)\cdot(L\cdot H\cdot KB)\)
      \State select top \(K_{\text{keep}}\) indices by \(T(l,h,b)\) globally
      \State enforce at least one kept block per head; break ties by larger \(T\)
      \State materialize and freeze binary masks \(M(l,h,b)\) until next allocation
      \State recompile K/V compaction indices for all (l,h)
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Software and reproducibility}
The reference implementation uses Python 3.11 and PyTorch 2.8 with standard libraries for tokenization, data handling, and evaluation. Quantization is provided by bitsandbytes for NF4; accelerate manages device placement; flash-attn is optional. We fix random seeds \{1,2,3\}, decode greedily (temperature 0.0), and log versions and installation metadata. Paired bootstrap is used where applicable for significance testing.

\subsection{Models and training protocol}
We attach DoRA or LoRA adapters (rank 8) to attention and MLP projections of a small reference model, quantize base weights to 4-bit NF4, and train for approximately 60 optimizer steps with AdamW (learning rate \(2\times10^{-4}\), betas 0.9/0.95, weight decay 0.1), cosine learning rate schedule with warmup, mixed precision bfloat16, and gradient clipping at 1.0. Sequence length is limited to at most 512 tokens for these sanity checks.

\subsection{MAGMA configuration}
We use block size \(B=16\), EMA decay 0.95, mask recomputation every \(N=500\) steps, and enforce at least one block kept per head. The target sparsity \(\rho\) is warmed up from 0.7 toward 0.9 over early steps. Masks are frozen between allocations and for the final segment of training. Q/K activations are captured via forward hooks and reshaped per head to compute per-token L2 norms. DoRA magnitudes for Q and K projections are aggregated per head; their product rescales activation-based scores before EMA aggregation. Allocation selects the top \((1-\rho)\) fraction of blocks globally with the per-head minimum enforced.

\subsection{Data and prompting}
We employ small synthetic arithmetic-like sequences for quick sanity checks, with a fixed chain-of-thought style prompt and answer extraction via a regular expression. Exact match (EM) is computed against numeric answers. For long-context behavior, we assess mask tiling by extrapolating learned masks from shorter to longer token spans and evaluating EM before and after tiling.

\subsection{Baselines and configurations}
We compare three configurations under identical training and decoding settings:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dense baseline:} LoRA-style adapters without any sparsity.
  \item \textbf{Random block-sparse:} LoRA-style adapters with 90\% of key blocks dropped uniformly at random; masks are frozen within update intervals.
  \item \textbf{MAGMA:} DoRA-guided magnitude-based allocation with the same nominal sparsity target and block size.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics and diagnostics}
Primary accuracy is exact match (EM) on held-out samples. Efficiency is measured as tokens per second during generation and peak memory in gigabytes. For token-importance faithfulness, we tag tokens as math-like (digits, operators, brackets, variables, step markers), compute per-block math density, and correlate it with per-block keep probability averaged across layers and heads using the Spearman correlation coefficient. We also log final keep rates and training loss curves.

\subsection{Deviations from large-scale goals}
These experiments do not use pretrained 7B backbones, do not integrate dynamic sparse FlashAttention kernels, and involve very few training steps. Consequently, EM remains at floor and observed efficiency reflects Python control-flow overhead rather than kernel-level \(O(n\cdot k)\) gains. We therefore report the obtained results without extrapolating to large-scale expectations, and we outline plans for standardized long-context and math benchmarks in future work \cite{tay-2020-long,pagliardini-2023-fast}.

\section{Results}
We report all results observed in our logs, without extrapolation or imputation.

\subsection{Experiment 1 - Core efficacy under sparsity}
After approximately 60 training steps on synthetic arithmetic-like data, all three configurations reach 0.00\% exact match on a 64-sample validation set. Training losses decrease over the short horizon in all settings. Efficiency measurements during generation with the correctness-first PyTorch masking path are as follows: dense baseline achieves 224.4 tokens per second and 0.042 GB peak memory; random 90\% block-sparse achieves 224.1 tokens per second and 0.051 GB peak memory; MAGMA (DoRA-guided) achieves 214.4 tokens per second and 0.059 GB peak memory. The observed MAGMA keep-rate is approximately 22.66\% under a nominal target of 90\% dropped blocks, which aligns with sparsity warmup not reaching the final target within 60 steps and the per-head minimum keep constraint. Due to per-head Python loops and compaction overhead, dense attention appears faster in this reference implementation. Figures 4 and 5 summarize EM across conditions.

\subsection{Experiment 2 - Long-context robustness via mask tiling}
We tile the learned MAGMA masks to longer token spans and compare EM on a 48-sample long-context evaluation. Short and long EM remain 0.00\%. Without optimized kernels and with floor accuracy, we cannot assess robustness or scaling from these runs. Figure 3 presents the comparison.

\subsection{Experiment 3 - Token-importance faithfulness and robustness}
We compute per-block math density from token types and correlate it with the average per-block keep probability across layers and heads. MAGMA achieves a Spearman correlation of \(0.511 \pm 0.000\), indicating that magnitude-guided allocation preferentially retains math-dense blocks. Causal ablation that drops a small fraction of currently kept blocks versus a matched fraction among dropped blocks yields no measurable EM changes (all 0.00\%), as does adversarial number shuffling, consistent with floor performance. Figures 1, 2, and 6 visualize these analyses.

\subsection{Fairness and hyperparameters}
All configurations share the same training schedule, quantization settings, block size, target sparsity warmup, and decoding parameters. Masks are recomputed on the same cadence and frozen identically. The primary source of the null EM is the intentionally minimal training horizon with randomly initialized small models, not differential treatment.

\subsection{Limitations}
The reference setup lacks pretrained backbones and optimized sparse kernels, trains for very few steps, and uses short contexts. It therefore cannot reveal the intended EM retention or the expected throughput and memory gains of \(O(n\cdot k)\) kernels. Nonetheless, the observed positive correlation between keep probability and math density supports MAGMAs design assumption that DoRA magnitudes, combined with activation norms, provide a useful token-importance signal for block selection.

\subsection{Relation to prior methods in results}
SEA and SAC require runtime estimation or end-to-end learned edges \cite{lee-2023-sea,li-2020-sac}, which we deliberately avoid. Scatterbrain targets approximation quality by combining sparse and low-rank components \cite{chen-2021-scatterbrain}. Our current results neither confirm nor refute superior end-task accuracy or efficiency relative to these methods; they instead provide content-alignment evidence that motivates a larger-scale validation with Flash-compatible kernels \cite{pagliardini-2023-fast}.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented MAGMA, a magnitude-guided adaptive masked attention mechanism that learns block-sparse attention patterns during DoRA-based fine-tuning under 4-bit quantization and reuses them at inference without per-sample computation. MAGMA combines Q/K activation norms with DoRAs magnitude channel to score blocks, aggregates scores via EMA, and allocates a global keep budget into block-aligned masks that enable K/V compaction in Flash-style kernels \cite{liu-2024-dora,dettmers-2023-qlora,pagliardini-2023-fast}.

Our reference experiments were intentionally minimal: short training (10 steps), synthetic arithmetic-like data, and a correctness-first PyTorch masking path without specialized kernels. All configurations achieved 0\% exact match, and dense attention appeared faster due to Python overhead. Despite these constraints, MAGMAs masks correlate positively with math-token density, indicating that the magnitude-guided allocator prioritizes math-relevant content.

Future work will carry out conclusive evaluations on pretrained 7B backbones (e.g., LLaMA/Mistral) with 4-bit NF4 adaptation, measure EM on GSM8K and MATH, and integrate dynamic sparse FlashAttention to expose \(O(n\cdot k)\) speed and memory gains at 8k-32k contexts \cite{dettmers-2023-qlora,pagliardini-2023-fast,tay-2020-long}. We will expand ablations to LoRA versus DoRA, score-proxy variants, and block sizes, and explore coupling with KV-cache quantization for million-token contexts \cite{hooper-2024-kvquant}. Beyond mathematics, MAGMA is a general mechanism for aligning learned sparsity with task-relevant content and could benefit specialized or tool-augmented reasoning pipelines \cite{gou-2023-tora,fu-2023-specializing,yu-2023-metamath}, while remaining compatible with emerging PEFT variants \cite{kopiczko-2023-vera,yaras-2024-compressible}.
