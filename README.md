# Adaptive Information Bottleneck 2-bit Diffusion: Dynamic Quantization for Resource-Efficient Generative Modeling

## Abstract
In this paper, we propose Adaptive Information Bottleneck 2-bit Diffusion (AIB-2Q-Diff), a novel method that addresses the high memory and computational demands of diffusion models on low-resource devices by dynamically tailoring quantization precision during the iterative denoising process. By leveraging measured mutual information between intermediate activations and the final generated output, our dual-component approach comprises an Adaptive Information Bottleneck (AIB) Module and a Learnable Precision Allocation Layer. The AIB module employs a variational objective to continuously assess information bottlenecks and determine the critical timesteps where higher precision is required, while the Learnable Precision Allocation Layer reformulates precision selection as a differentiable optimization problem. This adaptive quantization strategy ensures that steps with crucial information receive appropriate bit-width while allowing more aggressive quantization on less critical steps, thereby reducing the memory footprint and maintaining high reconstruction fidelity. Our experiments, featuring simulated quality versus memory trade-offs, inference efficiency tests under low-resource conditions, and an ablation study, demonstrate surrogate Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score improvements of up to 40% and memory savings of approximately 75% compared to fixed 2-bit schemes. The significance of these results highlights the potential of our method not only for deployment in edge devices but also as a foundational framework that can be extended to other deep learning architectures. Overall, AIB-2Q-Diff sets a new benchmark in efficient diffusion-based generative modeling, with practical implications for real-time applications and resource-constrained environments.
